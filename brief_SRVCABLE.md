# Forensic Equity Research Brief: Sarvottam Cables Ltd (SRVCABLE)
**As of:** 23 September 2026 | **Primary Exchange:** NSE | **Analyst Horizon:** Long-Term Retail

---

## 1. Snapshot
Sarvottam Cables Ltd (SRVCABLE) is a manufacturer of power cables, wires, and conductors, primarily serving power utilities and data-centre developers [3, 1]. For Q1 FY27 (ended 30 June 2026), the company reported revenue from operations of ₹1,248 crore (+18.0% YoY), an EBITDA margin of 11.2% (-170 bps YoY), and a Profit After Tax (PAT) of ₹82 crore (+5.9% YoY) [3]. The latest available share price was ₹1,012 on 9 August 2026 [1].

---

## 2. Bull Case
*   **Strong Order Book & Demand Visibility:** The company's order book stood at ₹3,900 crore as of 30 June 2026, a significant increase from ₹3,150 crore a year ago, providing robust revenue visibility. Demand from power transmission and data-centre projects remains strong, with data-centre cables expected to double their contribution to domestic revenue in two years [2, 3].
*   **Capacity Expansion & Growth Guidance:** A new plant in Bharuch, Gujarat, which will add approximately 30% to cable capacity, is on track to begin commercial production in Q3 FY27 and contribute from the second half of FY27. This expansion supports the company's FY27 revenue growth guidance of 15-17% [2, 3].
*   **Improving Governance (Reduced Promoter Pledging):** Promoter pledging has significantly decreased from 35% in March 2024 to a more manageable 4.1% of promoter holding as of June 2026. This reduction in pledged shares mitigates a key governance risk often associated with Indian equities [4, 8].
*   **Growing Export Contribution:** Exports contributed 21% to total revenue in Q1 FY27, up from 16% in Q1 FY26. This increasing international presence can diversify revenue streams and potentially offer better margin opportunities [3].

---

## 3. Bear Case
*   **Raw Material Price Volatility & Margin Lag:** Q1 FY27 EBITDA margins contracted by 170 basis points, primarily due to a sharp rise in copper prices. While two-thirds of the company's contracts include price-variation clauses, there is a 1-2 quarter lag in passing on these cost increases to customers, which can depress interim profitability [2, 3].
*   **Elongating Receivable Days:** Receivable days have increased from 78 to 96 over the last year, mainly attributed to slower payments from two large state utility customers. This elongation ties up working capital and impacts cash flow conversion, even though management reports no defaults [2].
*   **Material Contingent Liability (GST Demand):** The company has received a GST demand order of ₹46.3 crore (including interest and penalty) for FY2020-21 and FY2021-22. This amount represents a significant 56.5% of Q1 FY27's Profit After Tax (₹82 crore), indicating a material financial impact despite management's claim of "no material impact" [3, 7].
*   **Debt-Funded Capex:** The Bharuch plant capex of ₹620 crore is partly funded by term debt, although the current net debt-to-equity ratio is manageable at 0.35 times. Continued large capital expenditures could lead to increased debt levels and interest burdens [2].

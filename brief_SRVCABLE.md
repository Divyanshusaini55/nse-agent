# Forensic Equity Research Brief: Sarvottam Cables Ltd (SRVCABLE)
**As of:** 23 September 2026 | **Primary Exchange:** NSE | **Analyst Horizon:** Long-Term Retail

---

## 1. Snapshot
Sarvottam Cables Ltd (SRVCABLE) is a manufacturer of power cables, wires, and conductors, primarily serving power utilities and the rapidly growing data-centre sector [3, 2]. For Q1 FY27 (ended 30 June 2026), the company reported revenue from operations of ₹1,248 crore (+18.0% YoY), EBITDA of ₹140 crore (+2.6% YoY), and Profit After Tax (PAT) of ₹82 crore (+5.9% YoY) [3]. EBITDA margins contracted by 170 basis points to 11.2% due to raw material cost pressures [3]. The order book stood at ₹3,900 crore as of 30 June 2026 [3]. The latest available share price was ₹1,012 on 9 August 2026 [1].

---

## 2. Bull Case
*   **Robust Order Book & Data-Centre Growth:** The company holds a strong order book of ₹3,900 crore, driven by sustained demand from power transmission and data-centre projects [3]. Data-centre cables, currently 9% of domestic revenue, are projected to double in two years, indicating a significant growth avenue [2].
*   **Capacity Expansion & Funding:** A new plant in Bharuch, set to add approximately 30% to cable capacity, is on track to commence commercial production in Q3 FY27 (Oct-Dec 2026) [3]. This expansion is well-funded, with 70% of the ₹620 crore capex already spent, primarily through internal accruals and term debt, maintaining a healthy net debt-to-equity ratio of 0.35 times [2].
*   **Diversified Revenue & Export Momentum:** Exports contributed 21% to Q1 FY27 revenue, a notable increase from 16% in the prior year, showcasing the company's ability to tap into international markets and diversify its revenue streams [3].
*   **Improved Governance:** Promoter pledging has significantly reduced from 35% in March 2024 to a much lower 4.1% of promoter holding as of June 2026 [4, 8]. This deleveraging of promoter shares is a positive indicator for corporate governance and minority shareholder confidence.

---

## 3. Bear Case
*   **Raw Material Cost Pressure & Margin Lag:** Despite strong demand, EBITDA margins contracted by 170 basis points in Q1 FY27 due to a sharp rise in copper prices, the primary raw material [1, 3]. While two-thirds of contracts include price-variation clauses, there is a 1-2 quarter lag in passing on these cost increases, which could continue to depress interim margins [2].
*   **Elongating Receivable Days:** Receivable days have increased from 78 to 96 over the last year, primarily due to slower payments from two large state utility customers [2]. This ties up cash in working capital, potentially impacting the company's liquidity and cash conversion cycle, even if management reports no defaults [2].
*   **Material Contingent Liability:** The company has received a GST demand order of ₹46.3 crore (including interest and penalty) related to input tax credit for FY2020-21 and FY2021-22 [7]. This amount represents a significant 56.5% of the Q1 FY27 Profit After Tax (₹46.3 Cr ÷ ₹82 Cr), indicating a material financial risk despite management's claim of no material impact and intent to appeal [3, 7].

## 4. Open Questions & Source Discrepancies
*   **Direct Discrepancies:**

The figure from the official company filing (₹1,248 crore) is considered authoritative, indicating a likely transposition or reporting error in the news article.
*   **Governance & Materiality Inquiries:**
    *   What specific measures are being implemented to address the elongating receivable days, particularly with state utility customers, and what is the projected impact on the company's working capital and cash flow in the coming quarters?
    *   Further clarification on the specifics of the input tax credit dispute leading to the ₹46.3 crore GST demand, including the legal basis for the company's appeal and the estimated timeline for resolution, given its significant materiality relative to quarterly profitability.
